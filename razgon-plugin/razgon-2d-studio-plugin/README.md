# Razgon 2D Studio

Turn any voiceover into a fully animated, cinematic stick-figure video, with a **director pass** on top: the camera moves with intent (speed waves, long braking, parallax foregrounds), arrivals and hits have weight (springs, impacts, squash), fast shots get real motion blur, transitions are designed with both shots (`whip`, `push`, `iris`, `match`, `smash`), titles and key words are kinetic, and sound effects land on the visual events. Rules: `skills/make-2d-video/references/director-pass.md`; API: `engine/motion.js`.

Since 0.4.0 the film is **directed in episodes** (`references/storytelling.md`): one place, one through-action and one camera move per stretch, explanations that dive out of the story and back, a variety budget, and `tools/direction_check.py`, which fails slideshow patterns (shot-template registries, one shot per sentence, uniform rhythm, place ping-pong, repeated compositions, static holds) before the long render.

Since 0.6.0 the film is made of **long takes over meaning beats** you write yourself (`story/beats.json`): the camera travels through one world (pans, pushes, fly-ins) instead of cutting, about 1–4 cuts per minute, and every text fits its container (`maxWidth`). `direction_check.py` fails more than 6 cuts per minute or a median take under 5 s.

Since 0.5.0 edits are **clean**: film `overlays` keep key text on screen across cuts for its full reading time and fade it in and out, and `direction_check.py --video` fails pops (anything appearing or vanishing within one frame), jump cuts (cuts between near-identical frames), strobing and static holds.

Since 0.7.0 a project can be **watched live without rendering**: `node tools/live.mjs --project projects/<slug>` serves `live.html`, where the player draws the film in real time under the voiceover at reduced quality, with a draggable timeline numbered by frames, ±5 s and frame-step keys, and an automatic reload at the same moment whenever scenes, story or the engine change.

Since 0.8.0 `render.mjs --text-check` fails any text held cut by the frame edge, and badges keep themselves inside the frame.

Black stick figures with glowing rim light acting out the narration in moody 2.5D sets, giant face close-ups, freeze-frame "time stopped" moments, animated numbers and charts, blueprint-style explainers and low-poly 3D orbit shots, with generated music and sound effects.

Every frame is drawn by code and rendered frame by frame. No image or video AI is used, so characters stay consistent and text stays sharp. Visuals are invented for each script; the style stays the same.

## What you can say
- "Make a 2D video from this voiceover": runs the whole pipeline.
- `/new-video`: set up a video, timestamp the narration, plan the shots.
- `/video-styles`: see your own shots in 5 looks (Cinematic Dusk, Midnight Blue, Golden Hour, Neon Noir, Soft Storybook), pick one or design your own.
- `/render-video`: build and check the scenes, preview, render the final video.
- `/video-sound`: music moods, sound effects, ambience, final mix.

## Inputs and options
- **Voiceover** (any common audio format).
- **Script:** optional. Timestamped (SRT/VTT/JSON/TXT) or plain (timed automatically). Without a script, the voiceover is transcribed.
- **16:9 or 9:16**, **720p / 1080p / 1440p / 4K**, optional burned-in captions.
- **Music:** generated by mood, your own track, or none. **SFX:** 36 built-in effects, or your own files.

## First-time setup (automatic)
On first use, Claude copies the bundled studio into your project folder (`razgon-2d-studio/`) and installs:
- ffmpeg,
- Node.js packages (renderer, Three.js, fonts),
- a headless Chromium,
- Python's numpy, scipy and faster-whisper.

Whisper downloads its speech model on the first transcription. Roughly 1 GB of disk is needed in total.

## Render times
A small 2-core cloud machine renders 720p at about 2 frames per second (a 47-second video takes ~11 minutes). 1080p takes about 2× longer and 4K about 8×. A modern desktop is 3–5× faster. Long videos at 4K are best rendered overnight.
